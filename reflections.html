1. Conceptual Understanding & Dynamics

Reflecting on structure-function relationships in ecosystem ecology, trophic energy transfers, and biogeochemical nutrient cycling.

Diversity & Richness

Diversity: Refers to species abundance and evenness.
Richness: Refers to the total number of distinct species in a given community.

Holdridge Life Zone & Classification

Global forest classifications utilize indices like the Holdridge Life Zone model, governed by Annual Precipitation, Humidity, and Temperature. Higher environmental thresholds set limits, while lower values restrict species availability.

2. Evolution, Speciation & Relict Species

Exploring how evolutionary pressures, genetic variations, and geological isolation shape biodiversity patterns.

Speciation Mechanisms

Allopatric Speciation: Physical/geographic barriers isolate populations, causing them to evolve differently.
Peripatric & Parapatric: Speciation across specific peripheral niches or adjacent borders.
Sympatric Speciation: New species arise within the same geographic region due to environmental gradients, food specialization, or accidental mutations.

Types of Relicts

Taxonomic Relict: Only a specific subset of a lineage survives while relatives go extinct due to climatic pressures.
Geographic Relict: Species formerly widespread now restricted to small isolated pockets.
Climatic Relict: Persistence driven by microclimate controls.

3. Biogeography & Biodiversity Patterns

Understanding how Earth's tectonic dynamics and environmental stability govern species distribution across horizontal and vertical gradients.

Continental Drift & Plate Boundaries

Alfred Wegener's Continental Drift theory and plate tectonic boundaries dictate global biodiversity distribution and mountain formation.

Environmental Stability & Gradients

Biodiversity patterns are primarily driven by environmental stability. Unstable vs. stable biogeographic factors establish distinct horizontal and vertical species gradients.

4. Field Work Observations & Ecosystem Comparisons

Through our field visits we got a clearer picture of three very different ecosystems, understanding their main characteristics, adaptations, and threats.

A. Pashan Lake (Wetland & Aquatic Ecosystem)

Pashan lake is an artificial lake that serves as a rich ecological hotspot for various migratory birds.

Main Characteristics: An artificial wetland/reservoir ecosystem characterized by shallow lentic waters and emergent aquatic macrophytes.

B. Mayureshwar Wildlife Sanctuary, Supe (Grassland Ecosystem)

The grassland ecosystem in Supe has loose top soil due to low rainfall and aridity along with basaltic rock below it.

C. Mulshi (Montane Catchment & Forest Ecosystem)

Mulshi catchment area is an ecologically rich region that collects rainfall to feed the Mulshi Dam on the Mula river.
